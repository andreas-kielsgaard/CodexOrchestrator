# Cross-layer evidence ordering

## Observation

Repeated corrections clustered where one outcome crossed productive writers, durable facts, native DTOs, TypeScript decoding, read models, and UI. Representative work first passed narrower or synthetic checks, then exposed defects when an executable consumer gate finally ran. CLH-01 likewise reached a known proposal-initiation action without the Slice first deciding whether the Step could perform it, turning that planned path boundary into a mid-Step callback.

## Reader and cause

The reader owns detailed Slice decomposition, evidence ordering, launch readiness, and consequential Slice decisions. Existing guidance covered authority and decision gates generally, but did not require tracing the intended evidence path through product actions before launch.

## Revision

Trace the intended evidence path through consequential product actions and either authorize them inside the Step or hold them as entry gates. For cross-layer outcomes, map producer-consumer behavior and require the first local consumer evidence to be runnable. Gate independent evaluation on a coherent executable candidate unless evidence strategy is itself the earlier concern. Give a shared convergence document one owner after the product checkpoint stabilizes.

## Evaluation

This should expose extensibility, ordering, privacy, and negative-authority mismatches earlier without prescribing implementation or encouraging oversized work units. It also reduces shared-document churn without postponing documentation that is itself a required deliverable. The guidance remains limited to actual contract and convergence boundaries.
