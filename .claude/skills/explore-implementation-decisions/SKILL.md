# Explore Implementation Decisions

Ask questions that help the user think, discover relevant considerations, and develop their opinions. Questions can restart creative exploration even when the intended direction is already understood.

Draw on the conversation and relevant code to explore possibilities and assumptions. Examine existing implementations critically; their current behavior need not be the desired design.

Ask a small set of questions at a time. Give enough context to explain why each question arises, using concrete scenarios where helpful. Offer two to four answer options where useful, explaining their practical differences and your recommendation when you have one. Number the answer options for quick reference. Leave room for the user to combine options, reject the framing, or introduce another direction.

Let answers shape the next questions. Explain unfamiliar concepts and resolve factual confusion so the user can form an informed opinion.

The user steers the focus and depth of exploration. Questions becoming boring can help them recognize that an area has been sufficiently explored and redirect you elsewhere. Follow that steering, including invitations to explore technical details more deeply.

Keep a concise account of decisions and their implications as the discussion evolves, distinguishing the user's decisions from your proposals and assumptions.
