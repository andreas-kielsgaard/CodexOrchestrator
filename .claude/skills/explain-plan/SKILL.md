# Explain Plan

Assume the user has not read the plan, does not fully understand the problem, and has forgotten the surrounding context. They want an explanation that flows easily and helps them judge whether the plan seems reasonable or something deserves closer thought before proceeding.

Optimize for effortless comprehension rather than comprehensive understanding. Briefly establish the problem, the intended improvement, and the shape of the proposed change. Supply enough context for each point to make sense without requiring the user to remember earlier discussion or open the plan.

Make consequential assumptions, choices, and tradeoffs easy to notice. Explain their practical significance and your recommendation with just enough detail for the user to recognize where they might want to intervene. If nothing appears to need special attention, say so plainly. Compress detail without concealing consequential implications.

Design the visual reading experience as deliberately as the wording. Consider where the eye lands first, how information is grouped, and how easily attention moves through it. Use short, connected chunks, whitespace, and selective emphasis. A small comparison or diagram can help when it reduces the effort of understanding. Choose the presentation for this plan; avoid a rigid template, dense inventories, or visual decoration that adds work for the reader.

Deliver the explanation and any prompts for human input in the conversation. The response should stand on its own; files can provide optional depth for agents and review.
